Your stores have grown. So has the list of apps around your Shopify store.

Many retailers start on Shopify and do well there. Then the business adds a second and a fifth store, a few hundred brands, a site in another country and a trade program. Each step brings another app, another bill and another place where stock or customers can drift apart. This page sets Shopify Plus and RetailSpec side by side for that situation. Be fair first

Who should stay with Shopify Plus

Shopify Plus is a strong platform. For some businesses it is the right home, and moving would cost more than it returns.

OWN-BRAND, ONLINE FIRST

Brands selling mostly their own products online

If you make what you sell and most sales happen on your website, you don't need a catalogue of other brands or deep store operations. Shopify is built around that model.

APP ECOSYSTEM

Teams invested in Shopify apps and themes

If your team, your agency and your workflows are built on apps from the Shopify App Store and a custom theme, that investment has real value. Rebuilding it takes time.

CHANNEL REACH

Merchants who rely on Shopify's sales channels

If a large share of your sales comes through channels you manage from the Shopify admin, or you sell other brands' products through Shopify Collective [7], staying keeps that reach in one place.

PUBLISHED PRICE

Buyers who need a list price today

Shopify publishes Plus pricing, starting at $2,300 USD a month [1]. RetailSpec does not publish pricing yet, so pricing comes through a conversation.

Point of sale for several stores

Plus includes POS Pro for the first 20 locations, and up to 200 with Shopify Payments [1] [2]. On other plans, POS Pro is $89 USD a month per location [3].

Offline checkout takes cash and manual payments without internet. Offline card payments must be switched on in the admin, need a compatible reader, and Shopify advises reconnecting within 24 hours to reduce the risk of declines [4] [5].

Native POS in every store, with offline selling, a direct link to the card reader, end-of-day capture, blind drawer counts and a loss-prevention log.

Training and test registers for new staff. Panorama shows every device in every store on one map.
Inventory across locations and purchasing

Plus supports up to 200 inventory locations [2]. Purchase orders can be created in the admin, listing products, quantities, costs, payment terms and supplier details. Receiving and cost adjustments are handled through an inventory transfer [6].

Multi-location inventory with transfers. Purchase orders in multiple currencies, with landed cost. Receiving with the supplier's documents attached.

Cycle counts, kits and bundles, and serial tracking.
Multi-brand catalogue sourcing

Merchants add products in the admin or import them by CSV file [8]. Shopify Collective lets retailers import and sell products from other Shopify stores, and the supplier ships each order directly to the customer [7].

Brand Library: subscribe to a brand and its whole catalogue lands in your store, written, photographed and barcoded. Updates from the brand flow in. You hold and sell your own stock.
Selling internationally

Markets target specific countries or regions [10]. Local currency pricing needs Shopify Payments for full functionality [11]. Languages are added per market, and translation runs through the Translate & Adapt app, CSV files or third-party apps [12].

Plus includes up to 9 expansion stores. Products, settings and inventory are not synced between them, and apps are billed per store [9] [2].

Several storefronts, each with its own currency and language. Automatic translations. The console itself runs in five languages.
B2B and trade

Shopify B2B is available on Plus. B2B customers are set up as companies, each with its own pricing, currency, products, and payment and shipping methods [13]. Plus adds unlimited B2B catalogs [1] [2].

Trade and B2B accounts with their own price lists.
Shipping

Merchants can buy, print and manage discounted labels from Shopify-supported carriers in the admin. Carriers depend on location, and discounts vary by plan. Domestic return labels are available for locations in the United States or Canada [14] [15].

Built in: labels, rates, tracking and returns. No separate shipping app or carrier account to set up.
Email marketing

Shopify Messaging, formerly Shopify Email, sends email, SMS and WhatsApp campaigns and supports automations [17]. Up to 10,000 emails a month are free, then $1 USD per 1,000. SMS is charged separately [16].

Marketing email and automated flows built in. Customers are recognised across your stores and websites, so a store purchase and a web visit belong to the same person.
Loyalty

Shopify's own guide says Shopify POS doesn't have built-in loyalty tools, and points merchants to third-party loyalty apps [18].

Loyalty and referrals built in, shared by the counter and the websites.
Accounting sync

For QuickBooks Online, Shopify's help center says to install the QuickBooks Online app [19], which is published by Intuit [20]. Xero sync is offered by third-party apps in the Shopify App Store [21].

QuickBooks, Xero and Business Central sync built in.
Reporting

All plans include 200+ real-time reports and custom analytics [1]. Plus adds advanced sales, cohort, inventory and customer reports, with custom dashboards and exportable data [2].

Reports across stores and websites, including loss prevention and sales tax tracking. Sam, an AI-powered console copilot, helps staff find answers in the console.
Data ownership and export

CSV files can export products, customers, inventory, and admin and POS users. Orders and discounts can be exported too [8].

Each merchant has its own database. You can leave at any time with a full data export.
Typical app needs

Shopify's own pages point to apps for loyalty [18], for QuickBooks Online and Xero [19] [21], and for translation beyond the Translate & Adapt app [12]. Keeping inventory in step between expansion stores relies on third-party apps or an ERP [9].

POS, inventory, purchasing, shipping, email, loyalty, referrals, trade accounts, translations and accounting sync are part of RetailSpec. Payments run through your own Stripe account.
The quiet cost

What the extra apps add up to

Apps are a strength of Shopify. They also add up as a business grows. Here is what to count when you compare.

  • Subscriptions. Each app has its own plan and price, and many price by order volume. Add them up for your busiest month, not your average one.
  • Per-store billing. On Plus, apps are billed per store, so each expansion store can repeat the same app bills [9].
  • Sync between systems. Every app keeps its own copy of some data. Loyalty balances, stock counts and accounting entries can drift when a sync fails, and someone has to notice.
  • Training and support. Staff learn several tools, and a problem can mean several vendors to call.
  • Changes over time. Apps update on their own schedules. A change in one can affect the others.

We have not listed app prices here. Prices vary by app and plan, and they change. The Shopify App Store lists each app's current price.
